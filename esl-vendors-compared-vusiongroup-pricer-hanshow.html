VusionGroup (SES-imagotag) vs Pricer vs Hanshow vs AiESL: Electronic Shelf Label Vendors Compared

October 28, 2026·15 min read·AiESL Research
VusionGroup (formerly SES-imagotag), Pricer and Hanshow are the three largest listed electronic shelf label vendors, and each wins on something different: VusionGroup on scale and a full retail-IoT stack, Pricer on its infrared network and cloud platform, Hanshow on hardware breadth and price in grocery. AiESL is far smaller than all three. Where we differ is architecture: an open middleware and AI pricing layer that drives labels from several brands, for retailers who want to avoid single-vendor lock-in. We build ESL systems, so read this as an informed comparison, not a neutral one — every competitor fact below links to a public source.

This guide compares the four on what you can verify: public financials, installed base, wireless technology, software platform and openness. It does not rank label hardware, because spec sheets are not comparable without testing in your own stores. For the twenty-year history of why the industry split into closed and open camps, see open vs closed ESL systems; for the full market picture, see ESL market size 2026.

How Do Their Wireless Technologies Differ?

Pricer is the outlier: its labels talk over infrared light, not radio. VusionGroup and Hanshow built proprietary 2.4 GHz radio systems, and VusionGroup is adding Bluetooth LE. The wireless layer matters because it decides which labels can ever join your network — and therefore your switching cost.

ApproachWho uses itHow it worksWhat to test
Infrared opticalPricerCeiling transmitters send light pulses; each label’s photosensor flashes back to confirm. Avoids the crowded 2.4 GHz band shared with Wi-Fi.Coverage in high-ceiling, obstructed or outdoor-adjacent areas; transmitter count per store.
Proprietary 2.4 GHz radioVusionGroup (legacy labels), HanshowVendor gateways or access-point dongles talk to labels on a protocol only that vendor’s labels speak.Update time for a full store; coexistence with your Wi-Fi; gateway count (see our gateway capacity guide).
Bluetooth LE / ESL ProfileVusionGroup (VusionOX, EdgeSense); a growing set of chip makersThe Bluetooth SIG’s ESL Profile, co-led by SES-imagotag and Qualcomm, standardises label control over Bluetooth 5.4.Whether a vendor’s labels are certified to the open profile or only use Bluetooth with a private protocol.
Middleware above the radioAiESLEach brand keeps its own radio and gateways; an open MQTT middleware drives all of them from one console and API.Exactly which label brands and firmware versions are supported, in writing.

Sources: Pricer (infrared technology); Extreme Networks WiNG documentation (SES-imagotag 2.4 GHz proprietary RF); VusionGroup (VusionOX, EdgeSense); Alcatel-Lucent Enterprise certification datasheet (Hanshow 2.4 GHz controller); Bluetooth SIG (ESL Profile).

One honest caveat for our own approach: middleware does not make incompatible radios interoperable. It sits above them. If a brand is not on the supported list, middleware cannot drive it — so “which brands, which firmware” is the first question to ask us, too.

Does Vendor Size and Financial Strength Matter?

Yes — an ESL estate is a ten-year commitment, so the vendor has to be there for battery replacements, firmware and software support long after the rollout. On that measure the three listed vendors are well documented, and their 2025 results diverged sharply.

Size cuts both ways. A large vendor is a safer counterparty, but your chain is a smaller share of its business, and a single-vendor estate gives you less leverage at renewal. That is the trade-off the lock-in test in our manufacturer guide is designed to expose.

Where Is AiESL Weaker Than the Big Three?

Scale, references and field service. We have not run a deployment the size of Walmart’s or Sobeys’, and we do not publish audited financials. Ask us — as you would any vendor — who will install and support the system in your country, and for references of comparable size. If your board needs a vendor with a thousand-store reference in your country, one of the three listed vendors is the lower-risk choice. Where we are a strong fit — mixed estates, zero minimum order quantity, pricing and data kept in your own systems — is set out in where AiESL fits.

How Do You Run a Fair ESL Vendor Evaluation?

Use the same written criteria for every vendor, then make two or three of them prove it in a paid pilot. Brochure comparisons, including this one, cannot tell you how a system behaves on your shelves.

StepWhat to doPass/fail evidence
1. RequirementsWrite down store count, SKUs, price changes per week, integrations and data-ownership rules.A one-page brief every vendor answers in the same format.
2. Long list to shortlistScore vendors on our ten comparison criteria and the six openness questions.Two or three vendors with written answers, not slides.
3. Paid pilotTwo or three representative stores, including your hardest (freezers, large footprint).Full-store update time, failed-update rate, integration effort in person-days.
4. Five-year costHardware, software subscriptions, installation, batteries, support.Total per store over five years — see the cost guide.
5. Exit testAsk what happens to labels, data and templates if you leave.A written answer, ideally in the contract.
