AI dynamic pricing at the shelf edge — inside the guardrails you set

The AiESL Pricing Agent weighs demand, stock, margin targets and competitor signals, then recommends or automatically applies the right shelf price and pushes it to every connected label in under a second — with floor prices, approval rules and a full audit trail.

// THE PROBLEM

Why shelf prices lag behind the business

Prices move slower than demand

Demand, stock and competitor prices change daily; paper-based repricing runs weekly. The decision is ready long before the shelf is.

Markdowns arrive too late

Fresh stock is marked down at the end of its life, in one big step — or not at all — so margin and product are both lost.

Competitive repricing is manual

Category teams compare competitor prices by hand and re-tag in batches, so reactions come days after the market moved.

// HOW IT WORKS

A closed pricing loop, from signal to shelf in seconds

The AiESL Pricing Agent is an AI decision engine that weighs demand, stock, margin targets and competitor signals, then recommends or automatically applies the shelf price — and pushes approved prices to every connected label. It combines large language models with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), runs in a cloud-edge architecture and responds in about 350 ms; approved prices propagate store-wide in under a second.

// GUARDRAILS

Guardrails first: the AI proposes, your policy decides

Every recommendation is bounded by rules the engine cannot cross, and every change is logged. That is what makes automated pricing governable — and defensible to shoppers and regulators.

GuardrailWhat it does
Floor prices and margin limitsHard boundaries per product or category; no recommendation can breach them.
Approval modesRecommend-only, or auto-apply under auto-approval rules — set per category.
Audit trailWho or what changed each price, when, why and with which inputs.
Same price for every shopperThe engine works on product, stock and market data, not on individual shoppers’ data; everyone sees the same shelf price.

Regulation is moving in this direction: New Jersey’s 2026 Fair Price Protection Act bans individualised pricing based on consumer data. What the research says about ESLs and surge pricing — and what it says about markdowns — is summarised in are ESL labor savings overstated?
